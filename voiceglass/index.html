Show the work · methods, limits & privacy
  • The mic stays off until you press Start. When live, the most recent eight seconds are kept in RAM. Freeze releases the microphone, keeps that window, and unlocks scrubbing. Hearing a pin also releases a live microphone first. Audio analysis happens on your device. No audio or measurements are uploaded, and no recordings are saved. Reloading clears everything.
  • The inspector uses 64 ms of mono audio every 40 ms. Pitch is an estimate of the repetition frequency, not the brightest line. Blank or sudden octave jumps can mean the tracker lost a period, especially on creak. Fan hum, music or another speaker can look voiced too.
  • The spectrogram uses 64 ms (Harmonics) and 6 ms (Vowel bands) Hann windows, each on a fixed −80 to 0 dBFS display scale. The first two supported peaks of a 14-pole LPC envelope give tentative F1/F2 estimates. Two window lengths reveal different things: long windows separate harmonic lines; short windows blur them together to show broad regions of energy. Formants are resonances of the vocal tract, sampled by those harmonics. Gold markers are tentative band centers, not measurements of tract length; an isolated vowel is far easier than running speech or high F0.
  • Periodicity describes how closely cycles repeat. Noise, irregular phonation and a moving pitch all affect it. Upper/lower balance is the ratio of energy at 1.5–4 kHz to 0.2–1.5 kHz, in dB. Vowel, pitch, level, room and mic can all move it. Neither value isolates breathiness, fold contact or vocal weight.
  • Level is digital dBFS, not calibrated room loudness. The browser is asked to disable echo cancellation, gain control and noise suppression; hardware or OS processing can remain. Pitch span is the 25th-to-75th percentile interval of accepted F0 in semitones, not an expressiveness score. Pauses are timing, not mistakes.
  • Try a comfortable sound; if it feels scratchy or strained, stop. Higher, brighter and more periodic are not goals. Pick the sound you want to hear again.
